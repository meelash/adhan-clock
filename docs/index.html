Adhan Clock

A prayer-time clock you build from a Raspberry Pi Pico and a 64×32 LED matrix. It shows the time, the Hijri date and the day's prayer times, and plays the adhan from an SD card.

Download firmware Source on GitHub

MIT licensed · Raspberry Pi Pico (RP2040) · prebuilt firmware, no toolchain needed

Rotates every 5 seconds, like the real clock

What it does

Set it up once and it runs on its own: it keeps time through power cuts, follows daylight saving, and plays the adhan at each prayer.

Prayer times for anywhere

12 calculation methods, including MWL, ISNA, Umm al-Qura, Egypt, Karachi, Turkey and custom angles. Standard or Hanafi Asr, high-latitude rules, and per-prayer minute adjustments.

Adhan from an SD card

Plays any WAV recording you choose, with an optional separate Fajr adhan. Pick which prayers play it and set the volume. Any remote key stops it.

GPS time and location

With the optional GPS module, the clock sets its own time and coordinates. Without one, enter your latitude and longitude once in a text file.

Keeps time without power

A battery-backed DS3231 real-time clock keeps the time through power cuts. If the battery ever dies, the time turns red and prompts you to set it.

Hijri date and daylight saving

The Hijri date rolls over at Maghrib and can be shifted ±3 days to match local moon sighting. Daylight saving switches automatically on US or EU rules.

Colors that follow the day

Seven palettes, from amber at dawn to indigo late at night, change with the prayer periods. A light sensor dims the panel in a dark room.

Infrared remote

Change the time, calculation method, adjustments, adhan file, volume, brightness, time zone and more from the small remote. Your location goes in settings.txt, or comes from GPS.

USB drive mode

Choose USB Drive in the settings and the SD card shows up on your computer as a drive. Copy new adhans or edit settings.txt without removing the card.

The seven color themes

Same clock, different times of day. The theme changes at each prayer.

Parts

Three Waveshare boards and a Raspberry Pi Pico do most of the work. Apart from the SD breakout, nothing needs custom hardware.

1

Raspberry Pi Pico

The microcontroller that runs everything.

  • RP2040: dual-core Arm Cortex-M0+ at up to 133 MHz, 264 KB RAM, 2 MB flash
  • Get the Pico H (headers already soldered) so it plugs straight into the matrix board
  • The release firmware is built for the original RP2040 Pico
Product page
2

Waveshare Pico-RGB-Matrix-P3-64x32

The display, plus the carrier board the Pico plugs into.

  • 64 × 32 RGB LED panel (2,048 LEDs), 3 mm pitch, HUB75 interface
  • DS3231 real-time clock with backup battery holder (battery included)
  • Light sensor, buzzer, IR receiver, and buttons. Use it with the 21-key NEC IR remote
  • Pico sold separately
Product pageWiki
3

Waveshare Pico-Audio (PCM5101A version)

Plays the adhan.

  • PCM5101A stereo DAC over I2S, 106 dB SNR
  • Speaker output 2.6 W per channel into 4 Ω, plus a 3.5 mm headphone jack
  • The Rev2.1 board uses a CS4344 chip that needs an extra clock wire. It won't work with this wiring
Product pageWiki
4

Speaker

For the Pico-Audio speaker terminals.

  • One or two 4 Ω speakers rated about 3 W, or powered speakers on the 3.5 mm jack
  • The adhan is usually mono, so one speaker is enough
5

MicroSD breakout (SPI) and a microSD card

Holds the adhan recordings and settings.txt.

  • Any SPI microSD breakout. If it has a voltage regulator or level shifter, power it from 5 V (VBUS)
  • Card formatted FAT32. Cards over 32 GB usually come as exFAT and must be reformatted
  • Any size works: a 3-minute adhan at the recommended 22 kHz mono is about 8 MB
Example: Adafruit #254
6

Waveshare Pico-GPS-L76B Optional

Sets the time and location automatically.

  • GPS, BeiDou and QZSS, < 2.5 m accuracy, UART at 9600 baud
  • Holder for an ML1220 cell to speed up later fixes
  • Without it, set your coordinates in settings.txt and the time from the remote
Product pageWiki
7

Cables and power

Everything runs from the Pico's USB port.

  • Micro-USB cable and a 5 V USB power adapter. 2 A leaves headroom for the panel and speaker
  • Jumper wires for the SD breakout and Pico-Audio

Don't plug the Pico-Audio onto the header. Its audio pins are in the GP26, GP27 and GP28 positions, which the carrier board uses for the light sensor, buzzer and IR receiver. Connect those three pins with jumper wires to GP19, GP21 and GP17 as shown.
Don't press the carrier's K0, K1 and K2 buttons. They share GP15, GP19 and GP21 with the SD card and audio, and pressing one shorts that signal to ground. Use the remote instead.
Different wiring needs a rebuild. Pins are fixed in the firmware at build time. To use other pins, edit firmware/include/config.h and build from source.

Setup

No programming tools needed. You'll flash one file, then copy your adhan recordings to the SD card.

Flash the firmware

Download adhan_clock-<version>.uf2 from the latest release. Hold the Pico's BOOTSEL button while plugging it into your computer. A drive called RPI-RP2 appears. Copy the file onto it, and the Pico restarts as the clock.

Prepare the SD card

Format the card FAT32 and put your recordings in an adhan folder. They must be WAV files. 22,050 Hz mono sounds the same for a voice and is easy on the SD card. Convert an MP3 with ffmpeg:

ffmpeg -i adhan.mp3 -ac 1 -ar 22050 -c:a pcm_s16le makkah.wav

# the card:
/adhan/makkah.wav
/adhan/fajr.wav
/settings.txt        # the clock creates this on first boot

Set your location and time zone

With GPS, the clock finds its location and time by itself. You'll still set your time zone and daylight-saving rule. Do that with the remote, or edit settings.txt on a computer, either with the card in a reader or through the clock's USB drive mode:

# --- Location (decimal degrees, + = North/East) ---
latitude = 40.71280
longitude = -74.00600
gps_location = yes

# --- Time zone: standard (winter) UTC offset in hours ---
timezone = -5
dst = us

# --- Prayer calculation ---
method = isna
asr = standard

# --- Adhan audio (files in the /adhan folder) ---
adhan = makkah.wav
fajr_adhan = fajr.wav
adhan_prayers = fajr, dhuhr, asr, maghrib, isha
volume = 80

Use the remote

The clock is controlled with the IR remote that comes with the matrix board.

5Open settings / select
CH-Back / exit
28Up / down, or flip pages on the clock

To test the audio, choose Settings → Test Adhan. If something isn't working, Status shows GPS, SD card and RTC health at a glance.
